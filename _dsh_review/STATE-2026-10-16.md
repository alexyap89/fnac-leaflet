## The file is now generated

`index.html` is a build artefact. It was one 2.3 MB blob in which every edit had to be made
blind, which is why the previous review's regression (a handler bound to a `#btnRotate` that was
never added to the markup) could be introduced and uploaded without anyone opening the file.

    src/head_top.html      doctype/head/meta/title
    src/style.css          all CSS
    src/head_body.html     body markup
    src/lib/three.min.js   three.js r15x build, vendored verbatim
    src/app_glb.js         GLB parser, accessors, ROLE_COL
    src/app_scene.js       buildThreeScene(): scene, materials, skin, dressing
    src/app_app.js         UI, the 9-step machine, render loop, audio, controls
    src/tail.html          </script></body></html>

    python3 tools/build.py            rebuild index.html
    python3 tools/build.py --check    prove the fragments still reproduce what is on disk
    python3 tools/build.py --out P    build to P without touching index.html

`build.py` regenerates the model base64 from `src/manifest.json` ("model") and the narration
base64 from `audio/en/leaflet/step*.mp3`. Nothing is hand-edited into the page. A rebuild from
the fragments reproduces the shipped bytes exactly (verified: two consecutive builds are
byte-identical, and `--check` passes).

Tooling added: `tools/split.py` (regenerates `src/` from a shipped `index.html`),
`tools/glb_nodes.py` (GLB inventory and `--compare` node diff), `tools/build_bust.py` (Blender,
authors the bust), `tools/make_audio_leaflet.py` (narration).

## What changed

**1. The 3D skin was replaced.** It was a `LatheGeometry` body of revolution: per Y slice, the
viscera vertex furthest from the neck axis, smoothed and revolved. That is a vase — no head, no
jaw, no shoulders. It is now an authored bust of head + neck + shoulders, lofted from a
cross-section table and exported as `patient__bust` inside `model/fnac_scene_v9.glb`
(13,454 triangles, 41 cm tall, 36 cm across the shoulders). Built by `tools/build_bust.py`.

The fit is not guessed: the model is life size at **1 unit = 1 metre** (the comment in the old
file claiming "1 unit = 5 cm" is wrong), and the bust is tuned so the reported needle entry point
`(-4.62, 142.16, 4.90) cm` lies on the skin — it sits 6.1 cm from the neck axis, and the
sternocleidomastoid pair independently spans ±6.14 cm, so the neck is ~12.2 cm wide. Every
viscera mesh is enclosed by the skin; `_lab/check_enclosure.py` measures it.

Also fixed with it: the skin never exceeded 46% opacity, so even the steps that are supposed to
show the body surface rendered as dark brown glass. The ramp now reaches solid at steps 1, 4, 8
and 9 and fades only while the interior is revealed. The dressing, which used to be pushed
radially outward from a body of revolution and ended up as a white disc floating in space beside
the neck, is now docked onto the nearest point of the real skin surface along its outward normal.
An under-fill light was added because the jaw overhangs the throat and the key light alone left
that region as a black crease.

**2. The step sequence goes backwards.** `◀ Back` / `Next ▶` / a `N / 9` counter, disabled at the
ends, plus ←/→/Home/End on the keyboard (not while the canvas has focus, which already uses the
arrows to orbit) and horizontal swipe on the canvas. `▶ Play` now advances when the narration
clip for the step ends, falling back to the old 5.2 s timer only where there is no clip.

**3. The leaflet speaks.** It was silent: no audio element, no reference to the recorded files.
`audio/en/leaflet/step01..09.mp3` (0.79 MB total) are generated from the page's *own* step text by
`tools/make_audio_leaflet.py` — the text is parsed out of `src/app_app.js`, never copied, so the
voice cannot drift from the screen. This deliberately avoids the ten older clips in
`audio/en/step*.wav`, which were generated from a different ten-step script (`narration_en.json`,
titles "The neck", "The thyroid gland", …) that does not match this page's nine steps. Playback is
lazy per step, requires a real user gesture (browser autoplay policy), and a missing clip degrades
silently.

**4. Phone presentation.** The colour legend was `display:none` below 520 px, so the gland and
nodule key was lost on the device most patients will use; it is now a compact key in the stage's
top-right, clear of the step banner. Nav buttons are ≥44 px, chips are ≥40 px, the nav row sticks
to the bottom of the stage area with `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)`, and the long-form sections
were re-spaced on the same `--fs` scale.

## Still open

- **The bust still reads as a stylised mannequin, not a photograph.** The vision model still flags
  "does not look human" on 4 of 9 frames and a "seam where the head meets the neck" on 2. The
  front view at step 1 is the weakest; the interior views (steps 2–7) are the strongest and are
  where the explanation actually happens.

  **The route to fixing it properly, and where it got to.** `assets/Regions100.fbx` is a real
  human body surface, split into named anatomical regions, and it is in the same world frame as
  this model (verified: its trachea sits at z 1.36..1.46 and its thyroid at z 1.45..1.49, against
  y 1.355..1.461 and 1.4547..1.4888 here; anterior is −y there and +z here, scale 1, no offset).
  Rendering the assembled patches shows a genuine skull, jaw line and ear, so a convincing neck is
  reachable from assets already in this repo.

  It is not finished, and the reason is a trap worth writing down. The region patches are
  **parented into the FBX hierarchy**, each carrying `rot=[π,0,0]` plus a per-patch location, and
  `transform_apply` on a parented object bakes the local transform in while the parent still
  applies — the geometry gets the rotation twice and the surface moves (observed: max z 1.56 → 1.44
  m). Two more ways to lose a day: filtering patches by bounding box tears the surface wherever a
  region straddles the cut (join everything, then cut, never the other way round), and Blender's
  `bound_box` is stale after a `bmesh` edit, so it reports impossible numbers. A correct build
  needs: import, drop the ~8-vertex annotation stubs, `parent_clear(type='CLEAR_KEEP_TRANSFORM')`,
  join, weld, cut the chest plane, decimate, then swap it in for `patient__bust` — and no
  rotation, no axis swap and no fitting anywhere, because the exporter's own conversion plus the
  node transform already place it. Two abandoned attempts are in `/tmp/skin_mine.py` and
  `history://RealSkin`; the half-finished `tools/build_skin.py` that never produced a model was
  deleted rather than left in `tools/` looking authoritative.
- **Page weight is 3.76 MB**, up from 2.32 MB: 2.33 MB of model base64 and 1.05 MB of audio
  base64. It is a self-contained offline file, but it is no longer small for a phone on a slow
  connection. Dropping the narration to 32 kbps and decimating the bust further would recover
  roughly 1 MB.
- **The page's nine-step copy is still a variant that matches neither** `narration_en.json` nor
  `text_reviewed_current.json`, both of which hold a reviewed ten-step script used by
  `viewer.html`. The narration now matches the page's own nine steps, which adds no clinical
  claim, but which script is authoritative is a clinical decision, not an engineering one.
- `viewer.html` was not touched and still uses the older `assets/three.min.js`.
- The dressing is docked and visible at step 9 but still small relative to the neck.

# Round 2, 2026-10-17 — face, hair, orientation, narration, play-all, polish

Reported by the user after looking at the page: the face and neck looked backwards relative to
the gland and windpipe; there was no face and no hair; the narration had random pauses inside

## The bust was mirrored front-to-back — measured, then fixed

At the jaw band the bust reached z **−0.0943** posterior but only **+0.0713** anterior, while the
viscera put anterior at +z (thyroid −0.0045..+0.0370, tongue +0.0219..+0.0782). Cause: the loft
authored anterior as Blender **+y**, and Blender's glTF exporter maps +y to **−z**. Fixed by
mirroring the finished mesh in y and reversing the faces (a mirror inverts the winding, so
skipping that yields an inside-out surface). One mirror flips every cross-section and every
radial feature at once, which is why it is done to the mesh rather than by inverting axis signs
feature by feature.

Independently re-verified from the exported GLB, not from the builder's own report:
`chin_points_forward: false → true`, and `head_mass_same_side_as_tongue: false → true`. In the
page itself the trachea now runs down the front of the neck with the thyroid wrapping its upper
end and the nodule on it, directly under the chin.

## Face and hair

`tools/build_bust.py` loads **`_lab/face_spec.json`**, authored against this build by a local model
(mtplx / Qwen 27B): 25 radial features — brow ridge, eye sockets, nose bridge/tip/nostrils,
cheekbones, lips, mouth line, philtrum, chin, jaw angles, temples, zygomatic arches, ears — plus a
hairline definition. Face features are data, so the face can be re-specified without editing the
script. Two things had to be fixed afterwards: the nose was only about four vertices wide, so the
loft resolution went 128 → 256 angular samples and 4 → 3 mm vertically; and the spec's own hair
rows converge to a near-point, which produced a cone, so the cap now rides the **skull's own
profile** offset by 4 mm, which cannot intersect the head by construction. Three faint features
(eye sockets, brow, cheekbones, lips, chin, jaw) were deepened 1.7× because decimation erases the
shallow ones first — that is what turned the face from a smooth surface into a readable one.

## Narration: the pauses were the engine, not the punctuation

The clips had 5–6 silences each (measured with `ffmpeg -af silencedetect=noise=-35dB`) against
only 1–2 commas of real punctuation. First hypothesis was the 5 em dashes and 1 en dash in the
notes, and `tools/make_audio_leaflet.py` now normalises punctuation before synthesis — but
regenerating produced **byte-identical** audio, which disproved it: Google's engine normalises
dashes itself.

The real fix was the engine. The same text through macOS `say` gives **0–2 silences instead of
5–6**, and the longest gap drops from 0.48–0.56 s to 0.31–0.39 s. `say` runs fast (~175 wpm) and
the installed voice ignores `say -r`, so a pitch-preserving `atempo=0.86` sets the pace: the nine
clips land at 134–170 wpm, mean ~146. Bonus: `say` is entirely offline, so the narration no longer
depends on a network service, and the set is smaller (0.78 MB against 0.79 MB at the old bitrate).

## Play-all

`audioOn` defaulted to **false**, so `▶ Play all` never narrated and simply advanced on a flat
5200 ms timer — which is simultaneously "too fast" and the thing that cut a clip off if you turned
sound on part-way. Now: Play turns narration on itself and says so in the button; while a step has
a clip it advances **only** on that clip's `ended`; the timer path remains only for steps with no
clip or with sound off, at `2.0 + 0.055 × note length` seconds clamped to 3–14 s. Verified by
driving the page: at 10 s still step 1 with no clip ended, at 19 s step 2 with exactly one clip
ended, and Pause stops the clip without advancing.

## Polish

`src/style.css` was reworked against a design spec also produced by mtplx
(`_lab/design_spec.json`): a warmer near-black slate palette with a single amber accent replacing
the default-looking bright blue, an elevation scale, a radius scale, consistent hover/active/
focus-visible states across every control, accent-tinted selection, thin scrollbars, hairline
section dividers, a translucent 44 px step banner over the canvas, and `text-wrap: balance` on the
h1 to kill a widow. High-contrast ratios were re-checked against the new palette (worst case
10.3:1).

**A regression was caught here and fixed.** The hairline-divider rule added `padding: 0 24px` to
every `section`, which narrowed the stage from 960 to 912 px — 958×598 became 910×568, and at a
390 px viewport the 3D view fell from 348 to 300 px wide. It also re-created the header/body
misalignment the polish was supposed to remove. The padding was removed and the stage measures
958×598 again. Worth noting how it surfaced: the subagent reported "stage verified 958×598" from
box-model arithmetic, and it was wrong. Every dimension in this document is a measurement of the
rendered page, not a derivation.

# Cycle 3, 2026-10-17 — a face, eyebrows, eyes, and hair

Asked for: fix the badly placed hair, give the person a proper face (eyes, nose,
eyebrows, mouth, chin, cheeks), and have an adversarial reviewer, reading as a
patient, approve it.

## An adversarial patient gate now exists, and it is capable of saying no

`judge_batch` over the rendered views with patient rubrics — *would you be
reassured or bothered by this?* — plus `sees_eyes`, `sees_brows`,
`sees_face_parts`, `hair_ok`, and *what is the single worst thing?* It returns a
verdict of `approve`, `reject_unclear` or `reject_unsettling`. On the round-2
build it returned, correctly and with no prompting:

    reject_unsettling · eyes 0 · brows 0 · hair_ok 0 · worst "blank_face"/"smeared"

## What was built

- **Eyes, irises and eyebrows as real geometry.** A radial bump on a loft cannot
  make an eye, so `tools/build_bust.py` now builds the sclera spheres, iris
  discs and brow shapes as separate objects (`eye__ball_l/r`, `iris__disc_l/r`,
  `brow__l/r`) with their own `ROLE_COL` entries, kept out of the skin fade so
  they stay opaque at every step.
- **Face proportions corrected.** The eye line sat at z 1.560 on a head running
  chin 1.489 to crown 1.692, leaving a 13 cm cranium over a 7 cm face — a giant
  forehead with a small face stuck low. The eye group moved up 30 mm to 1.590
  (the midpoint, which is where an eye line belongs) and the nose 14 mm.
- **The face features were pruned from 30 to 21.** At this mesh resolution
  thirty overlapping bumps read as a clenched, lumpy face; the philtrum, the
  nasolabial folds, the canthi and the cheek hollows were removed and the rest
  capped.
- **The hair was wrong in four separate ways**, each found by measuring rather
  than looking:
  1. its rim was a two-point sinusoid between front and back, which parked the
     temples at the midpoint of the two and made it a swim cap → three
     waypoints now (front 1.665, temple 1.618, nape 1.558);
  2. its top came from the table's last row (1.688) while the skull's own top cap
     sat at 1.692, so the crown was bare and the hair perched on it → an explicit
     apex above the skull;
  3. it rode the **pre-subdivision table** while the skull is a subdivided mesh
     that fills out at the crown, so skin poked through → it is now ray-cast onto
     the actual built skull;
  4. that ray cast initially sampled the front of the head when it meant to
     measure the back, because the hair is built in the pre-mirror frame and
     `ray_cast` works in the mesh's post-mirror frame — worth writing down, as it
     inflated the cap into a balloon.
  The table also gained crown-closing rows: above 1.688 the profile was clamped,
  so anything sampling there got the same ellipse back and the hair rode it as a
  13 mm cylinder — the little spout on top of the head.

## The limit, and the two routes

Radial displacement features on a lofted cross-section stack plateau at a
stylised, slightly uncanny head. Ten builds were spent converging on that; more
parameter tuning will not cross it. Two routes would:

1. **The department's own surface.** `assets/Regions100.fbx` contains a genuine
   human body surface — rendering it shows a real skull, jaw line and ear — and it
   is in the same world frame as this model. Assembling it into a closed bust is
   the unfinished route recorded in round 2, with the trap written down.
2. **A sculpted head asset**, authored in Blender as mesh rather than as
   parameters, then dropped in as `patient__bust` + `hair__cap`.

Until one of those is done, the honest options for the shipped page are the
current face (a face with visible eyes and eyebrows, which the reviewer calls
unsettling) or the round-2 neutral bust (no facial features, which the same
reviewer called a blank face). The neutral one is arguably the safer default for
an anxious patient, since a clinical mannequin is familiar and a staring doll is
not — but that is a clinical judgement, not an engineering one.

# Cycle 4, 2026-10-17 — the user's own defect list, and the audio

The user looked at the page and reported six things, which turned out to be far more
useful than any of my own judgements: *the hair is funny; there is no mouth; there are weird
contours on the face; the neck is disproportionately long; the eyebrows are long and floating
above the face; the eyes are a tiny bit small.* Plus, separately: the dictation sounds
artificial, and the dash is not well dictated.

## Two real bugs behind three of those six

**The spec's table edits had never been applied.** `table_edits` and `table_delete` were
processed *after* the interpolation table had already been built from the unedited table, so every
one of them — the crown-closing rows, the raised shoulder line, the jaw changes — was silently
discarded. Proof rather than suspicion: the skull's apex measured 1691.9 mm before and after I
"added" crown rows at 1.696. Reordering so the interpolation is built after the spec's say moved it
to 1700 mm, and the shoulder/neck changes started to take effect. This is the second time an
ordering mistake in this file cost a cycle; the first was `shade_smooth` before subdivision.

**The nub on top of the hair was the skull, not the hair.** The cap closed its crown by *fraction
of the sweep*, which shrank the hair below the skull's own radius near the apex, so the skull's
crown poked through as a spike. It now closes by *height*: below the skull's top the cap stays at
skull + thickness, and only the last few millimetres above it converge. Measured radius profile at
the crown before this fix: hair 30 mm at 1685, 20 at 1690, 19 at 1695 — a 19 mm disc floating over
a skull that ends at 1692.

## The audio

**The dash was the culprit, exactly as the user said.** `speakable()` now works per sentence: a
*parenthetical* pair of dashes becomes commas, and a *lone* dash becomes a full stop with the next

## Four causes were eliminated by measurement before the real one was found

The defect survived every hypothesis I could cost-effectively test, and each test is worth
recording because each one is a trap that will come up again.

1. **Decimation.** Rebuilt with `BUST_DECIMATE` 0.20 and 0.60 (62k and 186k triangles). The crown
   profile came back **numerically identical** at both. Not the cause. It also disproves the general
   worry that the collapse pass is mangling the crown.
2. **The interpolation between table rows.** `_catmull` interpolates the profile to 3 mm rows.
   Catmull-Rom is not monotone-preserving, and the crown rows are 10 mm apart while the neck rows
   are up to 55 mm apart, which is exactly the arrangement that makes it overshoot its own control
   points. Replaced with a monotone cubic (PCHIP, Fritsch-Carlson tangents) - a real latent bug,
   correctly fixed, and it changed the profile by less than a millimetre. Not the cause.
3. **The Subdivision Surface.** Measured the built mesh against the table: at z 1.688 the mesh
   carried **27.8 mm of radius where the table specifies 60.8**. That is Catmull-Clark pulling the
   surface inside its control cage, unevenly, where curvature is highest - a large real defect.
   Removed, because the loft is already dense (3 mm rows x 256 samples) and shading smooth does the
   rest; the density is bought back by decimating less (0.50 instead of 0.20). Improved the crown
   substantially and did not remove the lid.
4. **The cheek-smoothing pass, which was my own bug.** A Laplacian pass over the face was extended
   to z 1.712 in an earlier cycle to fix a crown peak, and a Laplacian pass pulls a *converging*
   surface inward - the crown converges 15 mm per row. Its window now stops at the hairline
   (1.660). Again: real, and not the cause.

that is in fact smooth. The profile numbers above are from `toDataURL`-free geometry reads and from
the builder's own effective-table dump; the render is the ground truth for "is there a lid".

# Cycle 11, 2026-10-17 — one structural attempt, and why it was reverted

With the materials consistent, the remaining complaints are structural: the shoulders read as a flat

The cause of the plate reading was measured rather than guessed: the base row (z 1.256) is 0.178 m
half-width against a maximum of 0.182 m, so the widest five centimetres of the model vary by four
millimetres - a band of near-constant width reads as the edge of a disc, not as a deltoid. And the

## Cycle 14c — the graft built, and it is closed

The graft now exists and is a single closed manifold. Pipeline, in `/tmp/heads/graft2.py`:

1. Sculpt scaled to a 150 mm head, crown at 1.7064 m, cut at z 1.500.
2. Loft shoulders cut at z 1.365.
3. Both boundaries walked into **ordered loops** - 478 verts and 134 verts - rather than being
   approximated by a resampled ring. The first attempt lofted against 64-point resampled rings and
   left 478 and 134 real boundary verts dangling: 893 open edges, no amount of proximity welding
   closed them.
4. Rows interpolate centre **and** radius **and** the loops' own z, so the weld has something to
   bite. Using the cut plane's z instead of each vertex's real z missed the weld by fractions of a
   millimetre and left 37 open edges - a bug that only shows up because the seam is checked.
5. Two-loop stitch to close the 478-to-134 join.
6. **Voxel remesh at 1.6 mm**, not decimation. Collapse decimation tears a freshly welded seam
   (measured: 37 boundary edges before, **597 after**), because the stitch strip is all slivers. A
   voxel remesh guarantees one closed manifold and blends the seam into the neck at the same time.
7. Decimate **from** that clean manifold to 105,000 tris.

    boundary edges 0 | non-manifold edges 0 | 105,000 tris | GLB 1,890,944 bytes

## Cycle 15d — shortening the neck, and the route that tore the mesh

The user measured the problem precisely: the neck was "1.5-2x the length of a normal human's", with
the instruction to leave the trachea and thyroid alone.

**The route that failed, and it is the most important thing in this cycle.** Raising the shoulder
junction seemed obvious: with the crown fixed at 1.7064 and the chin near 1.50, a junction at 1.365
leaves ~13.5 cm of neck, so moving the junction to 1.405 and then 1.420 should have taken it to
~9.5 cm. It tore the model open instead.

The reason is in the source mesh, not the graft: the old bust is DECIMATED, and its neck is a smooth
cylinder there, so the decimator had collapsed it. The boundary loop the stitch has to join was
**628 vertices on the sculpt against 134 at 1.365 — but only 13 at 1.405 and 16 at 1.420**. Stitching
628 to 16 produces a mess of slivers, and the voxel remesh cannot close it: the result was a **hole
straight through the neck**, visible in the page as torn skin with the trachea and nodule showing
through. It was caught by looking at the render, not by a check — worth adding a guard for.

**The route that works.** Keep the junction at 1.365, where the loop is dense, and shorten the
visible neck with the BLEND CURVE instead: the trapezius exponent went from t^0.55 to **t^0.35**, so
the shoulders arrive much sooner up the neck. The result is closed — boundary edges 0,
non-manifold 0 — with every anatomical structure still enclosed, and the neck reads visibly shorter.

The entry point was re-derived for each of these builds by casting the needle's axis from the nodule
to the skin; this one is `(-0.0473, 1.4200, 0.0501)`, 2.2 mm from the value the original loft used,
which makes sense because the junction is back where it was.

## Cycle 20f - the parity question answered: my ray marcher double-counts

The open question from 20e - why parity on an identity-transform mesh disagreed with parity on the
same vertices after a GLB round-trip - is answered, and neither implementation was the problem.

Both matrices were **identity**, so the world/local theory was dead. Running every axis separately on
the same points gave the answer:

    z=1.4776  +X 1  -X 2  +Y 1  -Y 1  +Z 1  -Z 3     5/6 odd
    z=1.4657  +X 1  -X 1  +Y 1  -Y 1  +Z 2  -Z 1     5/6 odd
    z=1.4755  +X 0  -X 10 +Y 1  -Y 0  +Z 1  -Z 2     2/6 odd   <- nonsense

For a point inside a **closed** surface every axis must give an odd count - parity does not care
about concavity. Getting 0 in one direction and 10 in another from the same point is geometrically
impossible, so the marcher was wrong, not the mesh.

**The bug:** after a hit, the ray resumes `loc + dir * 0.0005` - half a millimetre past the surface.
On a large face a ray that grazes stays *within that face* for far more than 0.5 mm, so the next cast
hits the same face again, and again: counts inflate, parity flips, and the two implementations
disagree because they step along different axes and so meet the grazing condition differently.

**The fix, for the next attempt:** skip faces already hit (track indices) instead of stepping a fixed
distance, or step by the local triangle size. Until then **neither** enclosure figure from a parity
test is trustworthy - including the "20/61 outside" that sent three attempts back and the "ALL
INSIDE" that vindicated the fourth. The *frame* fix (20d) was real and stays; the parity method needs
this before any enclosure claim can be relied on again.

Shipped state is unaffected in its outward claims (it was verified by rendering as well as by this
check), but every enclosure number in this document should be read as provisional until the marcher
is fixed.

# Cycle 21, 2026-10-18 - "same issue, not resolved": the ramp never saturated

The user reported the neck issue unchanged after Cycle 20c claimed to fix it. It was unchanged, and
the cause was one line.

## The bug

Both files used this helper:

    def _ramp(a, b, z):
        if z <= a or z >= b: return 0.0      # <- wrong
        u = (z-a)/(b-a); return 3*u*u - 2*u*u*u

A ramp has to **saturate**: 0 below `a`, 1 at and above `b`. Returning 0 above `b` means the factor
jumps from 1 back to 0 at the top edge. The shrink factor was therefore:

    fully applied BELOW 1.415, ZERO above it

So the lower neck was 8% thinner and the whole upper neck was never touched - which is exactly the
user's "upper half is larger in diameter and lower is smaller", reported for the second time - and
the discontinuity at z 1.415 rendered as a hard ring. Measured on the shipped model before the fix:

    z 1413 -> 1416   width 117.27 -> 126.86 mm   <== STEP  +9.59 mm

It also explains why Cycle 20c changed nothing visible: moving the upper ramp to (1.545, 1.585) could
not help while the lower ramp shut the whole factor off above 1.415 regardless.

## Fix

    if z <= a: return 0.0
    if z >= b: return 1.0

applied in `integrate.py` and `loft2.py`. The shrink now genuinely covers the whole neck:
