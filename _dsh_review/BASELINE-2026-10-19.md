Three of the six complaints live in the page's own code, not in the model, and were changed there:

| fix | change | file |
|---|---|---|
| nodule must stand out | `ROLE_COL.nodule` `0x901727` -> `0xd94a15`; emissive `0xff4060` -> `0xff7a2a` | `src/app_glb.js`, `src/app_scene.js` |

| thyroid + trachea "slightly bigger" | thyroid grow `1.14` -> `1.26`; the trachea MESH scaled `1.15` about its own centre (it had never been scaled) | `src/app_scene.js` |

Why the nodule colour had to change: `0x901727` sits between the strap muscles (`0xa8544e`) and
skeletal muscle (`0x94443f`) - the palette is almost entirely reds and pinks, so a dark red nodule
could never separate from them. `0xd94a15` is the only strongly warm hue in it.

Why the size went in the page and not the model: `app_scene.js` ALREADY applies `grow(G.thyroid,
1.14)` and `grow(G.nodule, 1.30)` about their own centres for legibility, so the 48.6 mm thyroid
measured in the GLB is displayed at ~55 mm. Scaling it in `integrate.py` would have displayed it at
~64 mm and moved the enclosure numbers the geometry agent is holding clean. The geometry agent was
told to drop that item.

`build.py --check` OK): all three JS files pass `node --check`; the captured steps 1, 2 and 6 show a

## The neck-base holes: fixed

Two independent faults, found separately and both needed fixing.

**1. The cut (found by the local agent).** The old bust was cut by *deleting every vertex above
z 1.395*, which leaves a staircase boundary with vertices up to 8 mm off the plane; it met the loft's
planar bottom ring, the voxel remesh bridged the mismatch, and the resulting groove read as open
black slits. Replaced with `bisect_plane` on both pieces, so the cut is exactly planar (measured: the
boundary now sits at z 1.395..1.395, previously a spread of 8 mm) and the loft's bottom row is a
coincident copy of the shoulder's cut curve.

**2. The ring correspondence (found by me, after the fix above broke the model).** With an exact cut
in place the neck **pinched shut** - 7.5 mm wide at z 1.445 against ~120 mm either side. The loft
lerps ring A into ring B vertex-by-vertex, and the code paired them by reversing B by winding,
rotating B so its first vertex matched A[0], then resampling by arc length. The two walks start at
unrelated angles, so B came out rotated and every mid-loft row passed near the axis. A polar-angle
map failed too - the shoulder's cut ring is not convex, so a ray from the centroid crosses it more
than once and the first straddling edge lands on the far side (half the ring came back 180 deg out,
measured). **Nearest-point projection** has neither failure mode: no winding, no start alignment,
cannot wrap.

Verified in the render: the black slits around the neck base are gone (`/tmp/heads/chest_close_fixed.png`
against the earlier `chest_close.png`).

## Still open

- **The hair.** Still a smooth cap: a raised edge all round and a bump on the crown, which is the
  sculpt's own crown, faithfully copied. A taper of that edge was tried and **reverted** - pulling
  the ring in by a fixed percentage of the axis distance moves vertices by different amounts
  depending on how far out they already are, so the hairline tore into a zigzag. The real fix is the
  cap's form, not its edge.
- **Neck column 119 mm** against the 105 mm target. Improved by 29 mm, not there yet.
- **A soft crease** at the neck base measures 8.59 mm of width change over 5 mm (limit 8). It reads
  as a gentle ridge in the render, not the hard step the old ramp bug produced (9.59 mm), but it is
  over the line.
- **The chest has no landmarks** - no vertex anywhere is more than 0.5 mm below its 1-ring, so there
  is no sternal groove, no clavicle hollow, no trapezius. This is the largest remaining reason the
  model "still looks really off", and it is a sculpting job, not a patch.

## Correction: the pipeline is structurally, not bit-exactly, reproducible

I claimed earlier that rebuilding reproduced the page byte-for-byte. That was true of the file copy,
not of the pipeline. Re-running the whole chain from the same code gives a v12 of **identical size
(2,959,020 B) and a byte-identical JSON chunk** - same meshes, same accessor counts, same materials -
but a **different BIN chunk**: the float values differ slightly. The voxel remesh is multithreaded, so
the reduction order is not fixed. The result is geometrically equivalent (same topology, differences
well below the 1.1 mm voxel), but a re-run will not reproduce the shipped bytes.

The shipped artifact is therefore the one that was actually verified end to end, kept at
`/tmp/heads/bak-good-neckfix/` and restored from there.

## Two fixes attempted and reverted (both measured, neither shipped)

- **Crown smoothing** (`graft2.py`): the sculpt carries a bump on the crown that nothing smoothed -
  the band pass starts at 1.360 and the crown is at 1.60-1.70 - and the hair cap, being cut from that
  same surface, copied it faithfully. That is the "lump on top". Six smoothing passes over z >= 1.600
  removed the lump and kept containment clean, but made the **hairline visibly serrated**; A/B against
  the shipped render (`/tmp/heads/hair_ab.png`) shows a smoother dome traded for a worse rim, and the
  rim is the more visible fault. Reverted.
- **Wider junction relax** (`graft2.py`): to chase the 8.59 mm width change the check reports at the
  top of the neck band, the relax band was widened from 1.352..1.372 to 1.350..1.402. It made the
  figure **worse (10.13 mm)** - relaxing a wider band pulls the vertices at its edge toward their
  neighbours and sharpens the transition there rather than spreading it. Reverted.
- **Hair edge taper** (`hair.py`): pulling the extruded rim in by a fixed percentage of the axis
  distance tore the hairline into a zigzag. Reverted (recorded in the file).
